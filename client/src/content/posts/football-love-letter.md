## The Sport That Has Everything

I'm going to say something bold: football is the most complete sport on Earth.

Think about what it demands from you. Not one thing. Not two. *Everything.*

**Strength.** You're shoulder-to-shoulder with someone fighting for the same ball. You're shielding, pushing, holding your ground. A bad tackle can end your night.

**Endurance.** 90 minutes of running. Sprinting, recovering, sprinting again. Your lungs burn but you track back because your teammate needs cover.

**Technical skill.** A first touch that kills the ball dead. A through-ball threaded between two defenders. A chip over the keeper from 25 yards. Thousands of hours of practice compressed into one second of brilliance.

**Teamwork.** You can be the best player on the pitch and still lose if you play alone. The off-the-ball runs, the covering, the communication. Eleven brains thinking as one.

**Cold mind under pressure.** The ball lands at your feet in the 89th minute. One-on-one with the keeper. Your heart is slamming. The crowd is screaming. Can you stay calm? Can you pick your corner?

**Decision-making.** Pass or shoot? Dribble or release early? Press or hold shape? You have half a second. Every decision is permanent.

No other sport asks for all of these at the same time.

And look, I'll be honest: I'm a short guy. My body can't always keep up with what my brain wants to do. I'm never going to outmuscle a defender or win every aerial duel. But that's the beauty of football. You can play it with your brain. Read the space. Be in the right position before anyone else. One touch, release, move. Xavi was 5'7". Messi is 5'7". The game rewards intelligence as much as athleticism.

When you get it right, when everything clicks...

## The Dopamine Hit Nothing Else Gives

You know that moment? The one where you're running, you see the ball coming, your body just *knows* what to do, and you strike it perfectly? Or that last-ditch slide tackle where you get all ball and save a certain goal?

The dopamine is unreal. For those 90 minutes, nothing else exists. Not work. Not deadlines. Not your inbox. Not the PR that needs reviewing. You're 100% present. Your body is screaming but your mind is crystal clear.

I've tried running. I've tried the gym. I've tried cycling. None of them give me that same feeling. Because none of them have that *moment*. That unpredictable, unrepeatable instant where you do something beautiful and your friends go crazy.

Football gave me flow state before I ever knew what flow state was.

## 3 AM Champions League Nights

I have to tell you about university.

Every Champions League night, me and my friends would stay up until 3 AM. No question. Didn't matter if we had class at 8 AM. Didn't matter if we had exams the next day. When the anthem played, we were locked in.

After the match? We'd boot up Football Manager. Or fire up PES and play against each other until the sun came up. Talking tactics. Arguing about formations. Recreating the goals we just watched.

Those nights were some of the happiest of my life. Not because of the football itself, but because of the *people*. The shared obsession. The ritual. The feeling of belonging to something bigger.

## Then I Moved to Berlin

And slowly, without noticing, I let it all slip away.

It started with excuses. The pitches were too far. The groups I found online played at awkward times. The weather was terrible for outdoor football half the year. "I'll find a team next month." Next month became next season. Next season became a year.

A whole year without playing. Me. Someone who used to play three times a week without thinking about it.

## The Vietnam Ritual

The only time I touch a ball now is when I visit Vietnam. Every trip back, I text my old friends immediately: "When are we playing?" And within a day, I'm back on a pitch. Sweating in 35-degree heat. Lungs on fire. Legs dead after 20 minutes because I've been sitting at a desk for months.

But I'm smiling. Ear to ear. Even when I miscontrol a simple pass because my touch has gone rusty.

We play once, twice a week while I'm there. And for those weeks, I feel like myself again. Complete. Not "Phat the engineer." Just Phat.

Then I fly back to Berlin. And the sadness comes. Not dramatic, not depression. Just this quiet emptiness where football used to live.

## The Regret

Here's what I want to say clearly: **quitting football was the biggest mistake I've made in the last few years.**

Not because of fitness. Not because of health (though yes, sitting at a desk all day is slowly killing all of us). But because football was my social life, my stress release, my identity outside of work.

Without it, I became one-dimensional. Work, code, sleep, repeat. The thing that made me feel alive, that gave me stories and friendships and moments of pure joy? I traded it for convenience.

Don't do what I did. If you have a sport, a hobby, a thing that makes you forget time exists, protect it. Fight for it. Drive 40 minutes to the pitch if you have to. Play with strangers if your friends aren't available. Show up in the rain. Because the alternative is a year of "I'll start again next week" that turns into silence.

## What Comes Next

I'm not rushing into finding a team. That's how I'd burn out or pull a hamstring in the first session. After a year off, my body needs to remember what movement feels like.

So I'm starting small. Alone. A ball and an empty wall. Juggling in the park until my touch comes back. Short runs to catch my breath again. Nothing glamorous. Just rebuilding the foundation, rep by rep, until my legs stop feeling like they belong to someone else.

Then, when the fitness is there, when the touch is there, when I can sprint without wanting to collapse, I'll find the right opportunity. A casual group. A pickup game. Maybe eventually a team again.

And honestly? Part of me is thinking about something wild: becoming a part-time manager. Coaching. Tactics. Building a team. All those Football Manager hours have to count for something, right?

The beautiful game is coming back into my life. Slowly. Properly. On my terms.

See you on the pitch.
